## Q2 model answers

1. Basic research seeks general knowledge or theory without an immediate use requirement. Applied research uses systematic inquiry to solve a specific practical problem. **Marks:** each definition 1. *(Module 1)*
2. Check time, resources, ethics, data availability, and scope. A focused research question must be achievable within all five constraints. **Marks:** five checks 1; purpose 1. *(Module 2)*
3. Internal validity asks whether the observed effect is truly caused by the studied factor; external validity asks whether findings generalise beyond the study. **Marks:** each definition 1. *(Module 3)*
4. Nominal: blood group; ordinal: satisfaction rank; interval: Celsius temperature; ratio: age or mass. **Marks:** four scales 1; suitable examples 1. *(Module 4)*
5. Probability sampling gives population units known nonzero selection chances, enabling sampling-error inference. Non-probability sampling uses convenience, judgement, quota, or referrals and has unknown inclusion chances. **Marks:** each type 1. *(Module 5)*
6. Type I error rejects a true null hypothesis with probability α; Type II error fails to reject a false null with probability β. Power is `1−β`. **Marks:** each error 1. *(Module 8)*
7. State dataset/sample, comparison baselines, performance metrics, and statistical analysis. Together they make the proposed claim testable. **Marks:** four elements 1; purpose 1. *(Module 11)*

## Q3 model answers

1. Example: “Among university students aged 18–25, does using social media for more than three hours daily predict higher validated anxiety scores after six months compared with use of three hours or less?” This identifies population, exposure/comparator, measurable outcome, and time frame and can be tested observationally while controlling confounders. **Rubric:** population 1; exposure/comparator 1; outcome 1; time 1; coherent question 1. *(Module 2)*
2. Items rated 1–5 from strongly disagree to strongly agree: “Resources meet my needs,” “Staff are helpful,” “Study spaces are comfortable,” and “Digital access is reliable.” Reverse-code any negatively worded item. Pilot the scale and assess internal consistency with Cronbach's alpha (about 0.70 or higher is commonly acceptable), obtain expert review for content validity, and test construct validity against related measures. **Rubric:** four relevant items 2; response scale 1; reliability 1; validity 1. *(Module 4)*
3. Two rolls give `6×6=36` equally likely ordered outcomes. Sum 7 occurs for `(1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1)`, six outcomes. Thus `P(sum=7)=6/36=1/6≈0.1667`. **Rubric:** space size 1; outcomes 2; fraction 1; result 1. *(Module 7)*
4. Define the problem as excessive energy use and identify a gap in current profiling. Propose an analyser/optimisation, a representative app dataset, a strong baseline, battery/latency metrics, and paired statistical comparison. Include expected contribution, timeline, risks, reproducibility, user consent for telemetry, minimised/anonymised data, and secure retention. **Rubric:** problem/gap 1; method 1; evaluation 2; ethics/reproducibility 1. *(Modules 10–11)*
