# Module 2: Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35)

## Learning outcomes

- identify the six categories of Fundamental Rights;
- state the key articles (14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 21, 21A, 22, 23, 24, 25–28,
  29, 30, 32, 33–35);
- list the five writs under Article 32;
- explain the scope and restrictions on each right.

## Start here: the simple idea

Fundamental Rights are the basic human rights guaranteed to every citizen.
They are justiciable: if the government violates them, you can approach the
courts. Article 32 itself is called the "heart and soul of the Constitution"
(Dr. Ambedkar).

## Categories of Fundamental Rights

| Category | Articles | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Right to Equality | 14–18 | Equality before law; no discrimination; abolition of untouchability and titles |
| Right to Freedom | 19–22 | Speech, assembly, movement, residence, profession; protection against arrest |
| Right against Exploitation | 23–24 | Prohibition of trafficking and child labour |
| Right to Freedom of Religion | 25–28 | Conscience, profession, practice, propagation of religion |
| Cultural and Educational Rights | 29–30 | Minority rights to conserve culture and establish institutions |
| Right to Constitutional Remedies | 32–35 | Writs; Art 32 itself; power of Parliament to modify |

## Key articles explained

### Article 12 — Definition of "the State"

Includes Government and Parliament of India, State Governments, and all local
or other authorities within India or under the control of the Government of India.

### Article 13 — Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights

All laws inconsistent with or in derogation of Fundamental Rights are void.
"Law" includes ordinances, orders, bye-laws, rules, regulations, notifications,
customs, and usages.

### Article 14 — Equality before law

The State shall not deny any person equality before the law or the equal
protection of the laws.

### Article 15 — Prohibition of discrimination

No discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth.
Exceptions: special provisions for women and children; backward classes (SC/ST
and EWS).

### Article 16 — Equality of opportunity in public employment

No discrimination in employment under the State. Reservation allowed for
backward classes (Art 16(4)), SC/ST in promotions (Art 16(4A)), and EWS
(Art 16(6)).

### Article 17 — Abolition of Untouchability

Untouchability is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden; any
disability arising out of it is an offence.

### Article 18 — Abolition of Titles

No titles (not military/academic) to be conferred by the State. No citizen to
accept a foreign title without the President's consent.

### Article 19 — Protection of certain rights

Six freedoms under Art 19(1):
(a) freedom of speech and expression
(b) to assemble peaceably and without arms
(c) to form associations or unions
(d) to move freely throughout India
(e) to reside and settle in any part of India
(g) to practise any profession or carry on any occupation, trade or business

Each is subject to reasonable restrictions by the State in the interests of
sovereignty, security, public order, decency, morality, etc.

### Article 20 — Protection in respect of conviction

No ex-post-facto law; no double jeopardy; no self-incrimination.

### Article 21 — Protection of life and personal liberty

No person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to
procedure established by law. The Supreme Court has expanded this into a wide
range of rights (right to privacy, right to health, right to education, etc.).

### Article 21A — Right to education

Free and compulsory education for all children aged 6–14 years.

### Article 22 — Protection against arrest and detention

Right to be informed of grounds of arrest; right to consult a legal practitioner;
production before magistrate within 24 hours; safeguards for preventive
detention ( Advisory Board; max 3 months without Board opinion).

### Article 23 — Prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labour

Traffic in human beings, begar, and other forced labour are prohibited.

### Article 24 — Prohibition of employment of children

No child below the age of 14 years to be employed in any factory, mine, or
hazardous employment.

### Article 25 — Freedom of conscience and free profession, practice and propagation of religion

Subject to public order, morality, and health.

### Article 26 — Freedom to manage religious affairs

Religious denominations can establish and maintain institutions, manage affairs,
own property, and administer it.

### Article 27 — Freedom as to payment of taxes for promotion of any particular religion

No person compelled to pay taxes specifically for promoting any religion.

### Article 28 — Freedom as to attendance at religious instruction

No religious instruction in State-maintained educational institutions; consent
required for attending religious worship in aided institutions.

### Article 29 — Protection of interests of minorities

Any section of citizens having a distinct language, script, or culture has the
right to conserve it. No denial of admission into State or aided educational
institutions on grounds of religion, race, caste, language.

### Article 30 — Right of minorities to establish and administer educational institutions

Minorities (religious or linguistic) can establish and administer educational
institutions of their choice. The State shall not discriminate in granting aid.

### Article 32 — Remedies for enforcement of rights

Right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
The Supreme Court can issue five writs: **habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition,
quo warranto, certiorari**.

### Article 33 — Power of Parliament to modify rights

Parliament may restrict Fundamental Rights in their application to the Armed
Forces, intelligence organisations, telecommunication systems, etc.

### Article 34 — Restriction on rights while martial law is in force

Parliament may indemnify persons acting under martial law.

### Article 35 — Legislation to give effect to Part III

Parliament has exclusive power to make laws prescribing punishment for acts
violating Fundamental Rights.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing Art 19(1)(g) (profession) with Art 16 (public employment).
- Believing Art 32 can never be suspended (it can be during emergency, except
  Arts 20 and 21).
- Forgetting that Art 31 (Right to Property) was omitted by the 44th Amendment
  (1978); it is now a legal right, not a Fundamental Right.

## Memory rules

- FRs = Articles 12–35 (justiciable).
- Art 32 = writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto, Certiorari).
- Art 21 = life and personal liberty (expanded by judiciary).
- Art 21A = right to education (6–14 years).
- Art 24 = no child labour below 14 in hazardous work.
- Art 30 = minorities establish/administer educational institutions.
