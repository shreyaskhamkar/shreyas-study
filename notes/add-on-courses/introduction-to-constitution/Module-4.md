# Module 4: Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)

## Learning outcomes

- list all 11 Fundamental Duties;
- state which Amendment introduced them and when;
- explain the difference between Fundamental Rights and Fundamental Duties.

## Start here: the simple idea

Fundamental Duties are the moral obligations of every citizen towards the
nation. Unlike Fundamental Rights, they are not enforceable by courts, but they
serve as a reminder that rights come with responsibilities.

## Article 51A — Fundamental Duties

Added by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** on the recommendation of the Swaran
Singh Committee. Originally 10 duties; the **11th duty** was added by the
**86th Amendment (2002)**.

The 11 Fundamental Duties (Article 51A):

(a) To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the
    National Flag and the National Anthem.

(b) To cherish and follow the noble ideals which inspired our national struggle
    for freedom.

(c) To uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.

(d) To defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so.

(e) To promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all the
    people of India transcending religious, linguistic and regional or sectional
    diversities; to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.

(f) To value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture.

(g) To protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes,
    rivers and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures.

(h) To develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and
    reform.

(i) To safeguard public property and to abjure violence.

(j) To strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective
    activity so that the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavour
    and achievement.

(k) Who is a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to his
    child or ward between the age of six and fourteen years. *(Added by 86th
    Amendment, 2002.)*

## Important notes

- Fundamental Duties are **non-justiciable** (not enforceable by courts).
- They serve as a reminder that the exercise of rights must be accompanied by
  the discharge of duties.
- Art 51A is in **Part IVA** (added by the 42nd Amendment along with Part IVA).

## Common mistakes

- Thinking Fundamental Duties are enforceable by courts (they are not).
- Believing Art 51A was in the original Constitution (it was added in 1976).
- Confusing the 42nd Amendment (1976, 10 duties) with the 86th Amendment
  (2002, 11th duty on education of children).

## Memory rules

- 42nd Amendment (1976) → 10 Fundamental Duties + Preamble words "Socialist"
  and "Secular".
- 86th Amendment (2002) → Art 21A (right to education) + 11th Fundamental
  Duty (parent/guardian to educate children 6–14).
- Art 51A = non-justiciable; moral obligations of citizens.

## Answers

1. By the 42nd Amendment in 1976.
2. Eleven.
3. 86th Amendment (2002).
4. No, they are non-justiciable.
5. Article 51A(g).
