# Module 6: Obstacles to Human Rights and the Role of Advocacy Groups

## Learning outcomes

- identify major obstacles to human rights in India;
- explain how corruption and abuse of executive power affect human rights;
- describe the role of advocacy groups, NGOs, media, and educational
  institutions in promoting human rights.

## Start here: the simple idea

Even though laws and institutions exist, human rights are often violated because
of social, economic, and political obstacles. Good governance, transparency, and
active civil society are needed to overcome these obstacles.

## Obstacles to human rights in India

### Inequalities in society

| Obstacle | Impact |
|---|---|
| Population pressure | Strain on resources, reduced per-capita access |
| Poverty | Inability to exercise basic rights (food, shelter, health, education) |
| Caste | Discrimination, exclusion of SC/ST and OBCs |
| Inaccessibility of legal redress | Poor cannot approach courts; lack of legal awareness |
| Illiteracy | Unable to know or claim rights |

### Abuse of executive power

- **Corruption:** Misallocation of resources meant for welfare; denial of
  entitlements.
- **Nepotism and favouritism:** Unequal access to jobs and services.
- **Police brutality and custodial violence:** Violations of right to life and
  liberty.

### Good governance

Good governance = transparency, accountability, participation, rule of law, and
responsiveness. Poor governance is a major cause of human rights violations.

## Role of advocacy groups

### Media

- Investigative journalism exposes violations.
- Creates public opinion and pressures authorities.

### Lawyers and legal aid

- Provide free legal aid to the poor (Art 39A).
- File PILs on behalf of vulnerable groups.

### Educational institutions

- Impart Human Rights Education (HRE).
- Create awareness among students and the community.

### Corporate sector

- Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) contributes to welfare.
- Must respect human rights in operations and supply chains.

### Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs)

- Work at the grassroots to empower vulnerable groups.
- Document violations and advocate for policy change.
- Complement government efforts.

## Common mistakes

- Believing only the government is responsible for human rights (individuals and
  civil society also have roles).
- Confusing NGOs with government bodies (NGOs are independent).
- Thinking economic development automatically leads to human rights (it needs
  to be inclusive).

## Memory rules

- Obstacles: population, poverty, caste, illiteracy, corruption, bad governance.
- Advocacy = media, lawyers, educational institutions, corporate, NGOs.
- Good governance = transparency + accountability + rule of law.

## Check your understanding

1. Name two social obstacles to human rights in India.
2. How does corruption affect human rights?
3. What is the role of PIL in human rights enforcement?
4. Name two types of advocacy groups involved in human rights.
5. What is the link between good governance and human rights?

## Answers

1. Poverty and caste (also population, illiteracy, gender inequality).
2. Corruption diverts resources meant for welfare, denying entitlements to the poor.
3. PIL allows any public-spirited person to approach the court for violation of rights, especially for those who cannot approach the court themselves.
4. Media and NGOs (also lawyers, educational institutions, corporate sector).
5. Good governance ensures transparency, accountability, and rule of law, which are essential for the realisation of human rights.

## Quick revision box

- Obstacles: population, poverty, caste, illiteracy, corruption, abuse of power.
- Good governance = transparency + accountability + rule of law.
- Advocacy = media, lawyers/legal aid, educational institutions, corporate, NGOs.
- PIL = tool for public-spirited persons to seek justice for the vulnerable.
- Civil society + State = both responsible for human rights.
