# Module 2: Rights, Duties, and Legal Instruments

## Learning outcomes

- classify types of rights and duties;
- explain the relationship between rights and duties;
- list the main types of legal instruments (treaty, convention, covenant,
  declaration, protocol, etc.);
- distinguish between claim rights and liberty rights, positive and negative
  rights.

## Start here: the simple idea

A right is something you are entitled to; a duty is something you ought to do.
Rights and duties are two sides of the same coin — you cannot claim rights
without accepting corresponding duties.

## Types of rights

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Natural rights | Inherent in human beings by nature (life, liberty) |
| Legal rights | Created and enforced by the State |
| Claim rights | Entitlements that impose duties on others (e.g., right to education) |
| Liberty rights | Freedom from interference (e.g., freedom of speech) |
| Positive rights | Rights that require others to provide something (e.g., right to education) |
| Negative rights | Rights that require others to refrain from interfering (e.g., freedom of speech) |
| Individual rights | Held by individuals |
| Group rights | Held by groups (e.g., cultural rights of minorities) |
| Universal rights | Applicable to all human beings everywhere |

## Types of duties

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Natural duties | Derived from nature or conscience |
| Acquired duties | Arise from one's role or position in society |
| Positive duties | Require action (e.g., to help others) |
| Negative duties | Require abstention (e.g., not to harm others) |
| Perfect duties | Strictly binding; breach is always wrong |
| Imperfect duties | Loosely binding; depend on circumstances |
| Prima facie duties | Duties that appear binding unless overridden by stronger duties |

## Relationship between rights and duties

- Every right corresponds to a duty in others.
- Rights and duties are correlative: if A has a right against B, B has a duty
  towards A.
- The state has duties to protect rights; citizens have duties to respect others'
  rights.

## Legal instruments

| Instrument | Description |
|---|---|
| Treaty | Agreement between two or more States |
| Bilateral treaty | Between two States |
| Multilateral treaty | Between many States |
| Convention | A multilateral treaty, usually on a specific topic |
| Covenant | A binding treaty, often on human rights |
| Declaration | A statement of principles, usually not legally binding |
| Protocol | A supplementary agreement to a treaty |
| Conference | A formal meeting to negotiate or discuss |
| Resolution | A formal expression of opinion by an international body |
| Charter | A foundational treaty (e.g., UN Charter) |
| Statute | The fundamental law of an institution (e.g., Rome Statute of ICC) |
| Executive order | A directive issued by the executive authority |

## Common mistakes

- Confusing claim rights (entitlement requiring action by others) with liberty
  rights (freedom from interference).
- Believing declarations are always legally binding (usually not; covenants and
  conventions are binding).

## Memory rules

- Natural vs Legal = inherent vs State-created.
- Claim = require action; Liberty = require non-interference.
- Positive = act; Negative = refrain.
- Treaty = agreement; Convention/Covenant = binding multilateral treaties;
  Declaration = soft law.

## Check your understanding

1. What is the difference between a natural right and a legal right?
2. How are claim rights different from liberty rights?
3. Why are rights and duties correlative?
4. What is the difference between a treaty and a declaration?
5. What is a prima facie duty?

## Answers

1. Natural rights are inherent; legal rights are created and enforced by the State.
2. Claim rights require others to do something; liberty rights require others to refrain from interfering.
3. Every right implies a duty in others; they are two sides of the same coin.
4. A treaty/convention/covenant is binding; a declaration is usually a statement of principles without binding force.
5. A duty that appears binding unless overridden by a stronger duty in a specific situation.
