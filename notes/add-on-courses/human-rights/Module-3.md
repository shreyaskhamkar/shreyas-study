# Module 3: UN System and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights

## Learning outcomes

- describe the structure of the United Nations relevant to human rights;
- list the key UN Charter provisions on human rights;
- explain the significance of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR);
- recall the key provisions of UDHR Articles 1–30.

## Start here: the simple idea

The United Nations was founded in 1945 to maintain international peace and
security. Human rights became a central purpose: the UN adopted the Universal
Declaration of Human Rights in 1948 as a common standard for all nations.

## The UN system relevant to human rights

### Main bodies

| Body | Role in human rights |
|---|---|
| General Assembly | Discusses and makes recommendations; adopts declarations and conventions |
| Security Council | Can impose sanctions or authorise action for gross violations |
| Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) | Coordinates human rights work; oversees treaty bodies |
| Human Rights Council | Promotes and protects human rights globally; reviews member states |
| Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) | Leads UN human rights efforts |
| International Court of Justice | Adjudicates disputes between States (not individuals) |

### UN Charter provisions on human rights

- **Preamble:** Reaffirms faith in fundamental human rights, dignity and worth
  of the human person, equal rights of men and women.
- **Article 1(3):** One of the purposes of the UN is to achieve international
  cooperation in promoting and encouraging respect for human rights and
  fundamental freedoms for all without distinction.
- **Article 55(c):** The UN shall promote universal respect for and observance of
  human rights and fundamental freedoms.
- **Article 56:** All Members pledge to take joint and separate action in
  cooperation with the Organisation for achieving these purposes.

## Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)

- Adopted: 10 December 1948 by the UN General Assembly.
- Not a treaty, but a declaration of principles with enormous moral and political
  authority.
- It was the first global expression of rights to which all human beings are
  inherently entitled.

### Preamble of the UDHR

Recognises the inherent dignity and equal and inalienable rights of all members
of the human family as the foundation of freedom, justice, and peace in the
world.

## Common mistakes

- Believing UDHR is legally binding like a treaty (it is a declaration; its
  provisions were later turned into binding covenants — ICCPR and ICESCR in 1966).
- Confusing the UN Charter (1945, binding on members) with UDHR (1948,
  declaration).
- Forgetting that UDHR Art 29 explicitly mentions duties to the community.

## Memory rules

- UDHR = 30 articles, adopted 10 Dec 1948.
- First generation (civil-political): Arts 3–21.
- Second generation (economic-social-cultural): Arts 22–27.
- Third generation (solidarity): Arts 28–30.
- Art 1 = free and equal in dignity; Art 2 = non-discrimination; Art 29 = duties.

## Check your understanding

1. When was the UDHR adopted?
2. Is the UDHR a legally binding treaty?
3. Name the three generations of rights in the UDHR.
4. Which article prohibits discrimination?
5. What does Article 29 state?

## Answers

1. 10 December 1948.
2. No, it is a declaration of principles (soft law); later turned into binding covenants in 1966.
3. First generation: civil-political rights (Arts 3–21); Second generation: economic-social-cultural rights (Arts 22–27); Third generation: solidarity rights (Arts 28–30).
4. Article 2.
5. Everyone has duties to the community; rights may be limited by law for public order, public health, morals; no one may destroy the rights of others.

## Quick revision box

- UN founded 1945; UDHR adopted 10 Dec 1948.
- UDHR 30 articles = civil-political (3–21) + economic-social-cultural (22–27) + solidarity (28–30).
- Art 1 = free and equal in dignity; Art 2 = no discrimination; Art 29 = duties.
- UN bodies: GA, SC, ECOSOC, HR Council, OHCHR, ICJ.
- Declaration = soft law; Convention/Covenant = binding treaty.
