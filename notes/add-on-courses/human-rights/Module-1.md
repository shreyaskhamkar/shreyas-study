# Module 1: Introduction to Human Rights and Duties

## Learning outcomes

- define human rights and human rights education;
- explain the core values underlying human rights (dignity, liberty, equality,
  justice, ethics, morals, unity in diversity);
- state the three models of Human Rights Education.

## Start here: the simple idea

Human rights are the basic rights and freedoms that belong to every person in
the world, from birth to death. They are based on shared values like dignity,
liberty, equality, and justice. Human Rights Education (HRE) teaches people to
respect these rights and to discharge their duties.

## Core values explained simply

### Dignity

Every individual deserves respect. Dignity regulates behaviour and is the
foundation of liberty, equality, and freedom. The UDHR declares all individuals
are equal in dignity and rights.

### Liberty

Freedom to regulate one's own relations, behave at one's own will, and be
responsible for one's acts. Hobbes, Locke, and Rousseau articulated liberty as
a basis for rights. Excessive exercise without duty leads to problems; duty
protects liberty.

### Equality

All people are to be treated on the same footing without discrimination.
Formal equality = equal in the eyes of law. Substantive equality = concessions
for disadvantaged groups to achieve real equality.

### Justice

Righteousness and fair treatment on just and equitable grounds. Plato called
justice the highest value. Human rights aim to provide stable conditions so that
every individual can realise rights justiciably.

### Ethics and Morals

- Morals: personal character of an individual.
- Ethics: social system regulating the code of conduct of a group.
- Values: standard for deciding good/bad; shared by society.
- Ethics presupposes freedom as a necessary precondition.

### Unity in Diversity

People of different socio-economic, politico-cultural backgrounds must live
together compatibly under a single legal roof. The Indian concept of
*vasudhaiva kutumbakam* (the world is one family) expresses the same idea.

## Human Rights Education (HRE)

### Definition

> "A lifelong process by which people at all levels of development and in all
> strata of society learn respect for the dignity of others and the means and
> methods of ensuring that respect in all societies." — UN Decade document

### Three models

1. **Values and Awareness Model:** Transmits basic knowledge and fosters
   integration into public values through the curriculum.
2. **Accountability Model:** Focuses on professional responsibilities to monitor
   violations and advocate protection.
3. **Transformational Model:** Empowers individuals to recognise abuses and
   prevent them.

### Objectives of HRE

- Develop knowledge, skills, and values of human rights.
- Develop the socio-psychological human personality.
- Help people and policymakers evolve ways to overcome national and
  international problems.
- Foster understanding, tolerance, gender equality, friendship among nations,
  and eliminate racial, ethnic, religious, and linguistic differences.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking liberty means unlimited freedom without responsibility (duty regulates
  liberty).
- Believing HRE is only about rights, not duties.
- Forgetting ethics is social, morals are personal.

## Memory rules

- D-L-E-J-E-M-U = Dignity, Liberty, Equality, Justice, Ethics, Morals, Unity in
  diversity.
- HRE = Values + Accountability + Transformational models.
- Dignity is the foundation of all other values.

## Check your understanding

1. What are the six core values underlying human rights?
2. How do morals and ethics differ?
3. What are the three models of Human Rights Education?
4. What does "unity in diversity" mean in the human rights context?
5. Why is dignity called the most important basic value?

## Answers

1. Dignity, liberty, equality, justice, ethics, morals (and unity in diversity).
2. Morals deal with personal character; ethics regulates the social code of conduct of a group.
3. Values and Awareness Model; Accountability Model; Transformational Model.
4. People of different backgrounds must live together compatibly under a single legal roof.
5. Because the realisation of liberty, equality, and freedom depends on dignity.
