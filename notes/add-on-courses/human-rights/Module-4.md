# Module 4: Human Rights of Vulnerable and Disadvantaged Groups

## Learning outcomes

- define "vulnerable and disadvantaged groups";
- explain direct and indirect discrimination;
- list the major vulnerable groups covered in international law;
- identify the key conventions protecting women, children, disabled persons,
  minorities, and migrant workers.

## Start here: the simple idea

Vulnerable groups are people who face a higher risk of poverty, exclusion, or
abuse because of their social, economic, or cultural position. International law
extends special protections to these groups so they can enjoy human rights on an
equal footing with others.

## Meaning and concept

### Vulnerable groups

Groups that experience a higher risk of poverty and social exclusion than the
general population: ethnic minorities, migrants, disabled people, the homeless,
isolated elderly people, children, refugees, stateless persons, and victims of
war.

### Direct vs indirect discrimination

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direct discrimination | Treating a person or group less favourably on grounds such as sex, race, disability |
| Indirect discrimination | A practice that unintentionally disadvantages a particular group unless objectively justified |

Both involve harassment and create an intimidating, hostile, or degrading
environment.

## Major vulnerable groups and protections

### Women

- Protected under: Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination
  against Women (CEDAW, 1979).
- International Women's Day: 8 March.
- National Policy for Women (India).

### Children

- Protected under: Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC), 1989.
- UNICEF (United Nations Children's Fund).
- World Children's Day: 20 November.
- National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) in India.

### Scheduled Castes / Scheduled Tribes and indigenous peoples

- UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (2007).
- Indian constitutional safeguards: reservation, protection from exploitation.

### Aged persons

- Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007.

### Persons with disability

- UN Declaration on the Rights of Disabled Persons (1975).
- UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (2006).
- UN Enable organ.

### Minorities

- UN Declaration on the rights of ethnic, cultural, religious, and linguistic
  minorities (1992).
- National Commission for Minorities (India).

### Stateless persons

- UN Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees (1951), Article 1A.
- Statelessness: persons who do not possess the nationality of any State.
- Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs): forced to flee homes but remain within
  their own country.

### Migrant workers

- International Convention on the Protection of the Rights of All Migrant Workers
  and Members of Their Families (1990, in force 2003).
- ILO conventions: Migration for Employment (1949), Migrant Workers
  (Supplementary Provisions) (1975).
- National vs international migrant workers.

### Sex workers and HIV/AIDS victims

- Subject to special vulnerability; international efforts to protect dignity and
  health rights.

## Common mistakes

- Believing IDPs are refugees (IDPs stay within their own country).
- Confusing the various conventions and their years.
- Thinking vulnerability is only economic (it also includes social, cultural,
  disability, and other factors).

## Memory rules

- CEDAW = women (1979).
- CRC = children (1989).
- CRPD = disabled (2006).
- UN Declaration on Indigenous Peoples = 2007.
- Migrant Workers Convention = 1990 (in force 2003).
- IDPs = displaced but within own country; Refugees = cross border.

## Check your understanding

1. What is the difference between direct and indirect discrimination?
2. Name the convention protecting women's rights.
3. When was the Convention on the Rights of the Child adopted?
4. What is the difference between a refugee and an IDP?
5. Name the convention that protects migrant workers.

## Answers

1. Direct = less favourable treatment on prohibited grounds; Indirect = apparently neutral practice that disadvantages a group without objective justification.
2. CEDAW (1979).
3. 1989.
4. Refugees cross an international border; IDPs flee their homes but remain within their own country.
5. International Convention on the Protection of the Rights of All Migrant Workers and Members of Their Families (1990).
